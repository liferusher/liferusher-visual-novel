# Annotated Rewrites

Each example: a draft in a common failure mode, the rewrite, and what changed.
All examples are original, written for this repo.

---

## 1. The overstuffed opening

**Before**
```
My name is Rei Tanaka and I am a seventeen-year-old second-year student at
Sakuragaoka High School. I have brown hair and brown eyes and I'm pretty
average at everything. I live alone because my parents work overseas. Today
is the first day of the new school term and I am walking to school as I do
every day, thinking about how boring my life is.
```

**After**
```
    Three hundred and forty steps from my door to the school gate.
    >
    I know because I counted them in April, when I still thought counting
    things would help.
    >
    Today it's three hundred and forty-one.
    >
    Someone has parked a bicycle where the pavement used to be.
```

**What changed**
- Self-description deleted. The sprite and the story will handle it.
- "My life is boring" replaced with *evidence* of a boring life (counting steps)
  that also characterizes: precise, self-aware, quietly depressed, funny.
- Ends on an oddity — a hook. Something is different today.
- Four boxes instead of one. Four beats.

## 2. Narration echoing dialogue

**Before**
```
KAEDE: "I don't want to talk about it."
       Kaede said that she didn't want to talk about it. She seemed upset,
       and I could tell that she was hiding something from me.
```

**After**
```
KAEDE: "I don't want to talk about it."
       >
       She's already talking about it. She's been talking about it for
       twenty minutes, in the way people do when they say they aren't.
```

**What changed**
- Deleted the restatement.
- Deleted "seemed" and "I could tell" (filter words).
- Narration now *complicates* the line instead of confirming it — that's the gap
  (ch. 2).

## 3. Purple description

**Before**
```
The cherry blossoms danced upon the gentle spring zephyr like a thousand
delicate pink butterflies taking flight, and the ethereal beauty of the
scene filled my heart with an indescribable emotion that I could scarcely
put into words.
```

**After**
```
    Petals in the drain, petals on the bike seats, petals stuck to the
    inside of my collar where I'll find them tonight.
    >
    Every year I forget how much of it is just mess.
```

**What changed**
- Ornamental vagueness → specific physical detail.
- "Indescribable emotion I could scarcely put into words" — the writer refusing
  to do their job. Replaced with an actual, characterizing thought.
- The new version is funnier, sadder, and half the length.

## 4. Stage direction

**Before**
```
I turned my body toward the door and began to walk in that direction. When
I reached the door, I extended my hand and grasped the handle, then turned
it and pulled the door open. I stepped through the doorway into the hall.
```

**After**
```
    I go out into the hall.
```

**What changed**
- Everything the reader auto-fills is gone.
- If any part of that action mattered — a locked door, a shaking hand, a
  hesitation — that part and only that part would stay:
```
    I get as far as the handle.
    >
    ...Then I go and sit back down.
```

## 5. Emotion naming

**Before**
```
I felt a deep and overwhelming sadness. Losing her had made me feel
completely empty inside, and I knew I would never be happy again.
```

**After**
```
    I make two cups of coffee.
    >
    I notice at about the halfway point of the second one.
```

**What changed**
- Zero emotion words. The reader supplies all of it, which means they feel it
  instead of being told about it.
- Concrete, visual, and it's an image they'll carry.
- "Never be happy again" — melodramatic overclaim. Gone.

## 6. The tsundere with no motivation

**Before**
```
YUKI: "W-what?! It's not like I was waiting for you or anything, baka!
      Don't get the wrong idea! I just happened to be standing here! For
      forty minutes! In the rain! Hmph!"
```

**After**
```
YUKI: "You're late."
      >
      Her hair is flat against her head. The rain stopped ten minutes ago.
      >
      "I was in the area."
      >
      "The area."
      >
      "There's a vending machine."
      >
      There is not a vending machine.
```

**What changed**
- The denial is *quiet*, which is how denial actually works.
- The narration supplies the evidence, so the reader gets to be the one who
  notices. That's the pleasure of the trope.
- "Baka", stuttering, and "Hmph" — genre tics doing no work. Cut.
- Six boxes of rhythm instead of one wall.

## 7. Infodump

**Before**
```
ELDER: "As you know, the Concordance was formed three hundred years ago
       after the Sundering, when the seven great houses agreed to bind
       their magic to the Pillars in order to prevent another catastrophe.
       Each house contributes one Bearer every generation, and the Bearers
       maintain the Pillars until their deaths. This is why your family has
       served for nine generations and why you must serve as well."
```

**After**
```
ELDER: "Your grandmother lasted eleven years."
      >
      "...And my mother?"
      >
      "Your mother is why we're having this conversation early."
      >
      He pours the tea. He takes his time about it.
      >
      "Nine generations, Rei. You know the number. Everyone knows the number.
      Nobody ever asks what it's a count of."
```

**What changed**
- Same information density is *lower*, deliberately — the rest goes in TIPS or
  gets revealed later.
- Exposition attached to want and conflict. He's not lecturing, he's pressuring.
- The reader now has a question ("what is it a count of") instead of a summary.
- "As you know" construction eliminated.

## 8. Flat pacing in an action beat

**Before**
```
I heard a noise coming from behind me and I turned around quickly to see
what it was, and that was when I saw that there was a figure standing at
the far end of the corridor, watching me silently in the darkness.
```

**After**
```
    A sound behind me.
    >
    Not a loud one. That's the part I keep coming back to — how ordinary
    it was. Like a coat sliding off a hook.
    >
    I turn around.
    >
    The corridor goes on for about thirty metres.
    >
    Something is standing at the end of it.
    >
    It has been standing there for a while.
```

**What changed**
- One sentence → six boxes. The dread is *in the clicking*.
- Filter words ("I heard", "I saw") removed.
- Adverb ("quickly") removed; short boxes supply the speed.
- The last box withholds the explanation and supplies the worst detail.
- A tense slip into retrospect ("I keep coming back to") implies survival, which
  is its own kind of dread.

## 9. The over-long confession

**Before**
```
"I... um... I've been wanting to tell you something for a really long time
now, and I know this might be sudden, and I'm sorry if this makes things
weird between us, and you can totally say no, and I'll understand, and we
can just forget this ever happened if you want, but... um... the thing is...
what I'm trying to say is... I think... no, I know... I really, really like
you. A lot. Romantically. I'm sorry."
```

**After**
```
    "I need to say a thing, and I'm going to say it badly."
    >
    "Okay."
    >
    "...Okay."
    >
    A bus goes past. Then another one, which is unusual for this stop.
    >
    "I like you."
    >
    "Okay."
    >
    "That's it. That's the whole thing."
```

**What changed**
- Hesitation happens *twice* (the "...Okay.", the buses) instead of eleven times.
- The buses are a grounding beat that also functions as the pause.
- The response is minimal, which forces the reader to lean in.
- The last line is funny, in character, and defuses without deflating.
- The scene's real weight now lands in what comes *after* — which is where it
  belongs.

## 10. Route text that isn't actually different

**Before** — same scene, both routes:
```
[YUKI ROUTE]  We spend the afternoon at the festival. It's fun.
[KAEDE ROUTE] We spend the afternoon at the festival. It's fun.
```

**After**
```
[YUKI ROUTE]
    Yuki wants to do everything, in order, starting from the entrance.
    >
    We make it to stall four before she's out of money and I'm holding
    three goldfish.

[KAEDE ROUTE]
    Kaede finds the one bench at the back where you can see the whole
    thing without being in any of it.
    >
    We sit there for two hours. She explains the crowd to me like it's
    a documentary.
```

**What changed**
- The scene now characterizes. Readers running skip-mode will *stop* here.
- Same location, same event, completely different information about who these
  people are — which is what routes are for.
