# 10. Reference Works — What to Steal From What

An annotated study list. For each: what it does at the *wording* level, and the
specific technique worth taking.

Descriptions of style below are craft judgments, not consensus facts. Play or
read the relevant section before building on any of them.

---

## Tier 1 — read these first if you read nothing else

### Clannad / Clannad: After Story (Key, 2004)
**Genre:** nakige, slice of life → family drama
**Study for:** the comedy-to-tragedy pipeline; how mundane warmth becomes
devastating.

The famous structure: an enormous amount of low-stakes school comedy, written
with real affection and specificity, which makes the second half's losses land.
The prose itself is plain — that's the point. When it turns, it does not reach
for ornament.

**Steal:** the principle that *tragedy's power is proportional to how vividly the
ordinary was drawn*. Also: Key's use of a recurring motif (the light orbs, the
illusory world) as a structural spine across routes.

### Steins;Gate (5pb./Nitroplus, 2009)
**Genre:** sci-fi thriller with a comedic register
**Study for:** narrator voice, and comedy that carries plot.

Okabe's narration is a *performance* — the "Hououin Kyouma" persona is a
character trait, a running joke, and eventually a tragedy. The first several
hours are largely banter; the banter is doing characterization that the back
half spends.

**Steal:** give your narrator a bit they do. Let the bit break under pressure.
Also: the "@channel"/lab-member texture — modern, specific, dated-on-purpose
cultural detail.

### Doki Doki Literature Club (Team Salvato, 2017)
**Genre:** meta-horror disguised as a dating sim
**Study for:** genre-convention literacy, and UI-as-narrative.

Act 1 is a *correct* execution of standard dating-sim register — which is the
setup. The horror works because the conventions were reproduced faithfully first.

**Steal:** the principle that breaking a convention requires establishing it.
Also: its poem minigame is exceptional wording craft — the poems characterize
three people through diction alone.

### Umineko no Naku Koro ni (07th Expansion, 2007–2010)
**Genre:** meta-mystery, NVL
**Study for:** dense prose that still moves; unreliable narration at the
structural level; the reader-as-adversary framing.

Long, rhetorical, theatrical. It formalizes the mystery genre's "fair play" rules
*inside the fiction* (red truth / blue truth) and turns interpretation itself
into the plot.

**Steal:** the idea that you can give the reader explicit rules about what text
can be trusted, and then play within them. Extremely powerful and underused.

### Katawa Shoujo (Four Leaf Studios, 2012)
**Genre:** romance drama, English-native
**Study for:** English-language VN prose that isn't translationese; restrained
first-person interiority; route structure done cleanly.

**Steal:** its handling of protagonist interiority — Hisao is specific, flawed,
and self-deceiving in ways the narration quietly exposes. And its routes are a
clean model of common-route → five-route architecture.

## Tier 2 — specific techniques

### Higurashi no Naku Koro ni (07th Expansion, 2002–2006)
Convergent-arc structure; the same events re-read with new information. Prose is
plain-to-rough; the *structure* is the craft. **Steal:** question arcs / answer
arcs as a shape.

### 999: Nine Hours, Nine Persons, Nine Doors / Zero Escape (Spike Chunsoft, 2009–)
Branching made legible. Introduced/popularized the flowchart jump. Uses the
save-and-branch structure as an in-fiction device. **Steal:** the flowchart, and
bad ends written as real content.

### Danganronpa (Spike Chunsoft, 2010–)
Wildly distinct character voices — you could identify any of 16 characters from
one line. Also: trial dialogue as a *rhythm* form. **Steal:** the voice
fingerprint discipline (ch. 3).

### Ace Attorney (Capcom, 2001–)
Comedy timing at the text-box level is arguably the best in the medium. Nearly
every box has a beat. Also masterful at making exposition feel like conflict.
**Steal:** the box-level punchline discipline; the "objection" escalation rhythm.

### The House in Fata Morgana (Novectacle, 2012)
Literary register done properly; gothic, multi-era, tonally controlled. Proof
that dense prose can work in the medium when it's genuinely good.
**Steal:** motif work across centuries; restraint in horror.

### Saya no Uta (Nitroplus, 2003)
Short, unrelenting, utsuge/body-horror. **Steal:** its economy — it does in ~4
hours what many VNs fail at in 40. And its use of perceptual unreliability as a
premise rather than a twist.

### Fate/stay night (Type-Moon, 2004)
Chuunige register at its most influential — named techniques, declaimed
rhetoric, mundane-domestic scenes between fights. **Steal:** the contrast
discipline (cooking scenes between sword fights) and the quotable-declaration
style.

### VA-11 Hall-A (Sukeban Games, 2016)
Voice-driven, hub-structured, nearly plotless by design. The drink-mixing
mechanic *is* the dialogue system. **Steal:** how to make low-stakes
conversation carry a whole game; invented slang that stays consistent.

### Butterfly Soup (Brianna Lei, 2017)
Contemporary English-native teen voice; funny, specific, and completely
unconcerned with VN conventions. **Steal:** dialogue that sounds like recorded
speech; humor from character rather than from structure.

### Planetarian (Key, 2004) / Narcissu (stage-nana, 2005)
Kinetic novels. Short, linear, no choices. **Steal:** proof that you don't need
branching, and the discipline of a single controlled emotional arc.

### Analogue: A Hate Story (Christine Love, 2012)
Told through recovered documents and two AI interlocutors. **Steal:** non-scene
storytelling; the archive as a narrative form.

### Slay the Princess (Black Tabby Games, 2023)
Second person, fully voiced, branch-dense, theatrical. **Steal:** second-person
narration that *argues with the player*; a narrator as antagonist.

### Coffee Talk (Toge Productions, 2020)
Naturalistic, warm, low-conflict dialogue between adults. **Steal:** how to write
grown-ups; how to write comfortable silence.

### Roadwarden (Moral Anxiety Studio, 2022)
Second person, text-heavy, systems-driven. **Steal:** second-person description
that carries physical geography clearly.

### Muv-Luv / Muv-Luv Alternative (âge, 2003/2006)
The extreme case of tonal reversal across a trilogy. **Steal:** the audacity of
the structure; also a cautionary study in how long a setup can be before it's
too long.

### Little Busters! (Key, 2007)
Ensemble comedy with the strongest "group of friends" texture in the medium.
**Steal:** how to write a *group* scene where 6 people talk and it doesn't turn
to mush (answer: strict fingerprints + a clear scene goal + let some characters
be quiet).

### Grisaia no Kajitsu (Frontwing, 2011)
Sharp, adult-skewing banter; heroines with genuinely distinct psychologies.
**Steal:** dialogue that's funny and mean at the same time without being cruel.

## Non-VN reading that improves VN writing

- **Stage plays** (Pinter, Mamet, Sarah Kane) — dialogue-only storytelling, the
  pause as an instrument. VN scripts are much closer to plays than to novels.
- **Comics / manga** — panel-to-panel pacing maps almost directly onto
  box-to-box pacing. Scott McCloud's *Understanding Comics* is more useful to a
  VN writer than most prose-craft books.
- **Radio drama / audio fiction** — everything carried by voice and sound.
- **Screenwriting craft** — scene-goal/conflict/turn structure. *Save the Cat*
  is shallow but its scene-level questions are good ones.
- **Craft books:** Ursula K. Le Guin, *Steering the Craft* (sentence rhythm);
  Robert McKee, *Dialogue*; Sol Stein, *Stein on Writing* (for the cutting).

## How to study a VN rather than just play one

1. Pick a 20-minute scene.
2. Transcribe it box by box.
3. Label each box: dialogue / narration / thought / description.
4. Count boxes to the first turn.
5. Mark every place you clicked without learning something.
6. Find the scene's TURN and its last three boxes.

Do this three times and your own scene construction will change permanently.
