# 7. Genre Registers

Each VN genre has a wording contract with its reader. Breaking it deliberately is
a technique; breaking it accidentally reads as incompetence.

---

## Slice of life / comedy (*nichijou-kei*)

**Contract:** low stakes, high warmth, jokes every 30–60 seconds, characters
you'd want to have lunch with.

**Wording:**
- Short boxes, fast back-and-forth
- Narration is the straight man
- Specific mundane detail is the whole aesthetic (brands of juice, the broken
  vending machine, the exact seat someone sits in)
- Present tense, casual contractions, running gags

```
    The vending machine on the second floor eats my coin. Again.
    >
    I've lost, cumulatively, four hundred and eighty yen to this machine.
    >
    I keep using it because it's the only one that has the good melon soda.
    >
    It has never once dispensed the good melon soda.
```

**Study:** *Clannad* (school scenes), *Little Busters!*, *Katawa Shoujo*
(early chapters), *Coffee Talk*.

---

## Romance

**Contract:** the reader is here for the relationship's *texture*, not its
outcome. They know it works out. They want to feel each rung of the ladder.

**Wording:**
- Interiority does the heavy lifting — the gap between what's said and felt
- Physical proximity described in small, precise increments
- Escalating intimacy in *address* (see ch. 2)
- Silence beats used generously

```
Weak:  I realized I was in love with her.
Strong:
    She falls asleep on the train again.
    >
    Her head tips toward my shoulder, stops an inch short, and stays there.
    >
    I don't move for eleven minutes.
```

**Avoid:** confession scenes that are 40 boxes of "I— that is— what I mean is—".
Hesitate twice, then say it.

**Study:** *Katawa Shoujo*, *Clannad* (After Story), *Grisaia* (character work),
*Butterfly Soup* (modern, voice-driven).

---

## Nakige ("crying game") / utsuge ("depressing game")

**Contract (nakige):** make me laugh for fifteen hours, then destroy me. The
comedy is *load-bearing* — the loss hurts because the ordinary was drawn so
warmly.

**Contract (utsuge):** the bleakness is the point; don't promise a rescue.

**Wording:**
- Keep the baseline warm and specific. Every mundane detail is future ammunition.
- At the turn, **go plainer, not fancier.** The most devastating VN prose is
  usually the simplest.

```
Overwritten:
    An unbearable weight of infinite sorrow crushed my very soul as the
    cruel truth of her fate washed over me like a black tide.

Devastating:
    The nurse asks if I'm family.
    >
    I say yes.
    >
    Nobody corrects me.
```

**Study:** *Clannad* / *Clannad: After Story* (the genre's benchmark),
*Air*, *Kanon*, *Planetarian*, *Saya no Uta* (utsuge, body-horror register),
*Narcissu*.

---

## Mystery / thriller

**Contract:** information is currency. The reader is playing along. Fair play
matters — clues must be visible in retrospect.

**Wording:**
- Precision. Vague description destroys a mystery.
- Narration should *withhold* rather than lie (unless unreliability is the
  premise)
- Timestamps, floor plans, exact numbers, named objects
- End boxes on the pivot word

```
    The door was locked from the inside.
    >
    I know, because I locked it.
```

- Use TIPS/glossary systems to let obsessive readers re-check facts
- Chapter-end hooks are mandatory

**Study:** *Umineko no Naku Koro ni* (the meta-mystery ceiling),
*Higurashi*, *999 / Zero Escape*, *Danganronpa*, *Ace Attorney*, *Raging Loop*.

---

## Horror

**Contract:** dread is built by rhythm and restraint; the reveal is always less
frightening than the approach.

**Wording:**
- Short boxes. Very short boxes at the peak.
- Withholding breaks (ch. 4) are your main instrument
- Describe the *wrong* detail — not the monster, the thing next to the monster
- Narrator refuses to look / narrator's language degrades
- Silence: cut the BGM one box before the line

```
    There's someone standing at the end of the hall.
    >
    I say "someone" because it's the polite word.
```

- Avoid: gore inventory, exclamation marks, "suddenly", the word "terrifying"

**Study:** *Higurashi*, *Saya no Uta*, *The House in Fata Morgana* (gothic),
*Doki Doki Literature Club* (meta-horror, UI as instrument).

---

## Fantasy / sci-fi / chuunige

**Contract:** the world has rules and the rules are cool. Readers want
competence porn, escalating stakes, and quotable declarations.

**Wording:**
- Named techniques, named concepts, capitalized proper nouns — lean in
- Long declarative sentences at climaxes (this is the one genre where NVL-style
  rhetorical excess is *correct*)
- Contrast register: mundane banter between fights makes the fights hit
- Exposition through conflict, never through lecture

```
    "Then you already know how this ends."
    >
    "I know how it ends for one of us."
```

**Study:** *Fate/stay night*, *Dies Irae*, *Muv-Luv Alternative*, *Steins;Gate*
(sci-fi with a comedic register), *Tsukihime*.

**Caution:** chuunige register in a slice-of-life scene is the most common
accidental tonal failure in amateur VNs.

---

## Psychological / literary / experimental

**Contract:** form is content. The reader will tolerate slowness if the prose is
doing something.

**Wording:**
- NVL blocks, denser syntax, motif work, deliberate ambiguity
- The UI itself can be a narrative device
- Second person becomes viable here

**Study:** *The House in Fata Morgana*, *Analogue: A Hate Story*, *Slay the
Princess*, *Doki Doki Literature Club*, *Umineko* (later arcs), *Roadwarden*.

---

## Register cheat-sheet

| Genre | Sentence length | Ornament | Punctuation | Narration:Dialogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slice of life | Short | Low | Plain | 30:70 |
| Romance | Mixed | Low-mid | Ellipses, dashes | 40:60 |
| Nakige | Short at the turn | Very low at peaks | Plain | 40:60 |
| Mystery | Precise, medium | Low | Colons, dashes | 45:55 |
| Horror | Very short at peaks | Low | Sparse, no `!` | 50:50 |
| Chuunige | Long at climaxes | High | Em dashes, `!` | 50:50 |
| Literary | Long | High | Full range | 60:40 |
